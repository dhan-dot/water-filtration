Abstract

Water contamination is an escalating concern due to its impact on environmental ecosystems and human health. Water filtration technology provides a practical approach for reducing pollutants and improving freshwater quality; however, the effectiveness of different purification methods can vary depending on their mechanisms and individual techniques. This study evaluated five water filtration technologies: mechanical, ultraviolet treatment, reverse osmosis, granular activated carbon, and ion exchange resins. The purpose of this study was to determine which method most effectively improved the quality of freshwater samples collected from a local source. In this case, this experiment tested water at Ashburn Park Creek in Ashburn, Virginia. Twenty-five independent water samples were collected and tested, each of 200 milliliters, with five samples assigned to each filter. The water quality was assessed before and after filtration using turbidity measurements and drinking water quality test strips. Changes in the water quality indicators were analyzed with the use of a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) to determine whether significant differences existed among the five filtration methods. It was hypothesized that the granular activated carbon filter would demonstrate the greatest improvement due to its ability to adsorb various chemical components within the filter.

1. Introduction

Water pollution poses a significant threat to aquatic ecosystems and human health by introducing contaminants that degrade water quality, as well as increase the risk of diseases globally. One widely used approach of improving water quality is through the implementation of water filtration systems. Previous studies have demonstrated that boiling water alone may not sufficiently address all water quality concerns and health requirements [1]. As filtration technologies have advanced, water filtration systems have become increasingly common for household and outdoor water treatment. Different filtration technologies remove contaminants through various chemical and physical processes. Mechanical filtration removes larger suspended particles through physical barriers, while granular activated carbon (GAC) reduces chemical contaminants through adsorption. Reverse osmosis (RO) systems use a semipermeable membrane to remove dissolved substances, and ion exchange resins target specific charged particles, such as minerals and metals. Unlike traditional purification methods, ultraviolet (UV) treatment uses radiation to inactivate microorganisms rather than physically removing contaminants. Overall, water filtration removes impurities and contaminants from water, continuously making it safer for drinking, cooking, or other various domestic uses [1].

Environmental contaminants, including heavy metals, pathogens, and industrial chemicals, contribute significantly to diseases worldwide by reducing water quality and increasing exposure to hazardous substances [2]. Human activities such as urbanization, industrialization, mining, and exploration have contributed greatly to worldwide environmental pollution and increased the release of contaminants into water sources [3]. Water contamination remains a widespread issue affecting both local communities and global populations. Concentrations of at least one chemical contaminant were greater than an established MCL in 23% of the 1,389 wells [4]. These findings demonstrate the importance of developing accessible and effective purification methods, particularly for individuals who rely on untreated freshwater sources.

Although previous studies have evaluated individual water filtration methods, relatively few have directly compared multiple filtration systems using water collected from the same local source under controlled conditions. Many studies focus on the performance of a single filtration technology, making direct comparisons between different purification methods overall difficult. Evaluating multiple filtration approaches under identical conditions allows for a more accurate assessment of their effectiveness, as well as reducing environmental variance within the testing. The results of this study may help individuals identify effective portable purification methods for improving local untreated freshwater quality. The present study aims to determine which of five water filtration methods most effectively improves the quality of freshwater collected from a local creek. The five purification methods evaluated include mechanical filtration, ultraviolet (UV) treatment, reverse osmosis (RO), granular activated carbon (GAC), and ion exchange resins. We hypothesized that the granular activated carbon filter would demonstrate the greatest improvement in water quality due to its ability to adsorb a wide range of chemical contaminants. This study compares the effectiveness of five filtration methods by analyzing changes in water quality measurements before and after filtration.

2. Methods

Five filtration methods were evaluated, with five independent trials conducted for each method (n = 25 total trials). The independent variable was the type of filtration method used, while the dependent variable included changes in turbidity and water quality measurements before and after filtration. Controlled variables included sample volume, collection location, and testing procedures. Each trial consisted of a separate freshwater sample collected from the same source to allow for direct comparison among purification methods. Water samples were collected from a single location within Ashburn Park Creek in Ashburn, Virginia, during one collection period to minimize environmental variation among samples and improve consistency between trials. The filtration methods evaluated included mechanical, UV treatment, RO, GAC, and ion exchange resins.

To evaluate the effectiveness of each filtration method, water quality was measured using a turbidity sensor and drinking water quality test strips. Turbidity measurements were used to assess changes in water clarity, while water quality test strips were used to evaluate changes in chemical properties of each sample. Filtration materials, a turbidity sensor, and drinking water quality test strips were obtained prior to sample collection. Twenty-five separate water samples, each containing 200 mL, were collected from Ashburn Park Creek under the same environmental conditions. Prior to filtration, each water sample was tested using a turbidity sensor and drinking water quality test strips to establish baseline measurements. Each sample was then filtered using one of the five assigned purification methods, with five samples tested per filtration method. Samples were randomly assigned to filtration groups to reduce potential experimental bias. Following filtration, the same turbidity and water quality measurements were repeated and recorded. Differences between pre-filtration and post-filtration measurements were used to evaluate changes in water quality following each purification method.

All experimental data was recorded in Google Sheets. A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed using the online statistical software Statistics Kingdom to determine whether differences among the five filtration methods were statistically significant.
