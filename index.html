Abstract

Overview & Research Context

  • Global Concern: Water contamination poses an escalating threat to both natural aquatic ecosystems and human health.
  • Practical Solution: Advanced water filtration technology offers an accessible, practical approach for reducing pollutants in freshwater.
  • Varied Mechanisms: The effectiveness of purification methods can vary significantly based on their unique physical or chemical processes.
  • Local Focus: This study evaluates untreated freshwater collected directly from Ashburn Park Creek in Ashburn, Virginia.
  • Core Purpose: To determine which of five distinct filtration technologies provides the greatest overall water quality improvement.

Methodology & Experimental Design

  • Tested Technologies: Evaluated Mechanical, Ultraviolet (UV), Reverse Osmosis (RO), Granular Activated Carbon (GAC), and Ion Exchange Resins.
  • Sample Volume: Collected and tested twenty-five independent freshwater samples, each standardized to exactly 200 milliliters.
  • Replicate Design: Assigned five independent water samples to each of the five filtration methods (n = 5 per group).
  • Quality Testing: Assessed water quality before and after filtration using digital turbidity sensors and drinking water test strips.
  • Statistical Evaluation: Analyzed changes in water indicators using a One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) to evaluate statistical significance.

Hypothesis & Projected Outcomes

  • Primary Hypothesis: Granular Activated Carbon (GAC) will yield the greatest overall improvement in freshwater quality.
  • Hypothesis Rationale: GAC features high surface area porosity optimized for adsorbing a broad spectrum of chemical contaminants.
  • Baseline Evaluation: Initial pre-filtration testing established baseline turbidity and chemical parameter levels across all samples.
  • Comparative Goal: Identify the most effective portable purification method for individuals relying on raw freshwater sources.
  • Final Status: Experimental trials complete; final post-filtration statistical conclusions are currently being compiled.

1. Introduction

Water Pollution & Filtration Fundamentals

  • Ecosystem & Health Threat: Water pollution introduces harmful contaminants that degrade aquatic health and increase global disease risks.
  • Inadequacy of Boiling: Previous research demonstrates that boiling water alone cannot sufficiently eliminate all chemical pollutants or meet safety requirements [1].
  • Household & Outdoor Use: Modern advanced filtration systems have become essential, accessible tools for residential and field purification.
  • Mechanistic Diversity: Filtration relies on varying processes: mechanical (physical barrier), GAC (adsorption), RO (semipermeable membrane), and ion exchange (charged particle binding).
  • Disinfection vs Physical Removal: Unlike physical barriers, UV light employs radiation to inactivate microorganisms without removing physical particles [1].

Environmental Contaminants & Public Health

  • Hazardous Exposure: Heavy metals, pathogens, and industrial waste significantly increase disease vulnerability worldwide [2].
  • Human Activity Impacts: Urbanization, industrialization, mining, and exploration drive increased toxic runoff into natural freshwater bodies [3].
  • Widespread Impact: Untreated water contamination remains a critical global issue, directly impacting local and municipal water supplies.
  • Well Water Contamination: USGS research shows 23% of 1,389 tested domestic wells exceeded Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCL) for at least one chemical [4].
  • Need for Accessible Purification: Highlights the urgent necessity for simple, highly effective purification methods for individuals using raw water sources.

Research Gap & Study Objectives

  • Comparative Research Gap: Few existing studies directly compare multiple filtration systems using identical freshwater sources under controlled conditions.
  • Controlled Testing Advantage: Evaluating five methods simultaneously minimizes environmental variance and improves measurement accuracy.
  • Target Application: Helps individuals identify optimal, portable purification methods for raw freshwater in real-world environments.
  • Study Focus: Directly compares Mechanical, UV, RO, GAC, and Ion Exchange performance on local stream water.
  • Core Working Hypothesis: GAC will achieve the highest overall score due to its superior capacity to adsorb chemical impurities.

2. Methods

Experimental Setup & Controls

  • Sample Size (fn = 25): Evaluated 5 filtration methods with 5 independent trials conducted per method.
  • Variables: Independent variable was filter type; dependent variables were changes in turbidity and chemical test parameters.
  • Controlled Factors: Sample volume (200 mL), collection location, testing procedures, and environmental conditions were strictly standardized.
  • Collection Site: All samples were collected during a single timeframe from Ashburn Park Creek in Ashburn, Virginia.
  • Minimizing Bias: Samples were randomly assigned to treatment groups to eliminate experimental selection bias.

Testing Procedures & Data Collection

  • Clarity Measurement: Employed a digital turbidity sensor to quantify pre- and post-filtration water clarity.
  • Chemical Profiling: Utilized drinking water quality test strips to record chemical parameter shifts across each trial.
  • Baseline Recording: Measured turbidity and chemical metrics for all 25 raw samples prior to running through filters.
  • Filtration Execution: Filtered each 200 mL sample using its assigned method (Mechanical, UV, RO, GAC, Ion Exchange).
  • Post-Test Assessment: Repeated identical turbidity and chemical measurements immediately following filtration.

Statistical Analysis Protocol

  • Data Logging: Recorded all raw baseline and post-filtration measurements in Google Sheets.
  • Metric Shift Calculation: Calculated net change for turbidity and drinking water tests.
  • Analytical Software: Used the online statistical platform Statistics Kingdom to execute data analysis.
  • Hypothesis Testing: Performed a One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) across all five treatment groups.
  • Significance Evaluation: Assessed p-values to determine if filtration performance differences were statistically significant.
